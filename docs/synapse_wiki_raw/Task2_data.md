The **BraTS-PEDs 2025 Challenge** provides the largest annotated publicly-available  retrospective cohort of high-grade glioma (including astrocytoma and diffuse midline glioma (DMG)/diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma (DIPG)) in children. Conventional multi-parametric MRI (mpMRI) scans that are routinely acquired as a part of clinical brain tumor imaging protocol for pediatrics were manually annotated to be used in the training, validation, and testing cohorts. For all subjects, ground truth annotations of the tumor sub-regions were approved by expert neuroradiologists with years of experience in the field of pediatric neuron-oncology. These ground truth annotations are used to quantitatively evaluate the predicted tumor segmentations generated by the submitted algorithms. 

The **BraTS-PEDs 2025 Challenge** dataset comprises multiparametric MRI (mpMRI) scans, which include the following sequences:
* **pre-contrast native T1-weighted (T1N)**
* **contrast-enhanced T1-weighted (T1C)**
* ** T2-weighted (T2W)**
* **T2-weighted Fluid Attenuated Inversion Recovery (T2-FLAIR or T2F)**

**_Important notes on pre-processing:_** The BraTS-PEDs dataset is only defaced and not skull-stripped. If you need to skull strip the images, you may use our open-access model provided at one of the following repositories:
* https://github.com/d3b-center/peds-brain-seg-pipeline-public
* https://github.com/d3b-center/peds-brain-auto-skull-strip
We caution the participants about using skull-stripping in training their models, as the validation and testing data are also only defaced and not skull-stripped.

Participants are provided with:
* training dataset with associated ground truth labels
* validation dataset without associated ground truth labels
* testing dataset is kept hidden from participants at all times

**_NEW:_** A key addition to this year's challenge is an evaluation of inter- and intra-rater variability. To assess the consistency and reproducibility of manual annotations, two experienced annotators have segmented 25 cases four times: twice from scratch and twice revising the pre-segmentations generated using an nnU-Net model. This evaluation aims to provide insights into the variability inherent in manual segmentation, which is crucial for understanding algorithm performance in the context of expert uncertainty. By incorporating this analysis, BraTS-PEDs seeks to refine segmentation benchmarks and enhance the reliability of automated methods in pediatric brain tumor analysis.

###! Data Access

In addition to registering for the challenge, you must also request access to the data:

1. **Submit the form:** complete the [Data Access Google form](https://forms.gle/UiCpXos2zKFPdMnK6). Only one access form is needed across all 5 challenge tasks and their training + validation datasets\*.

2. **Accept your invite:** once your details are verified, the BraTS Service Account will email you an invitation to join the @BraTS2026DataAccessTeam . Accept it to unlock the files below!
